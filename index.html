    What we found, 12 October 2026

    We went, we queued, and we wrote down what happened.

    30minin the entry queue, with no waiting time displayed and no information along it.
    0recommended routes in the orientation room. A map, a screen, a scale model, lockers, and nothing telling you how to spend the afternoon.
    16:00the main food outlet had closed, while visitors were still on the estate.
    29%of the two million people on this estate last year went inside the château.
    0things for a child who turns up without a booking. The bee trail is hard to follow and rewards nothing.

    The best rooms in France, and nobody in them.

    The Galerie François I on an ordinary afternoon. Long labels, uneven translations, and no reason to stop walking.

    Sixty metres of salamander, and nobody is ever told.

    François I put his badge on every wall of this gallery in 1528 and gave it a motto. It is the mascot the Château already owns, and action 1 is simply to use it.

    An empty courtyard is a marketing problem, not a historical one.

    20 April 1814. Napoleon says goodbye to his army, on these steps.

    Everything that makes this place worth the fare already happened here. None of it is on the path a visitor actually walks.

    So: ten places to put it back.

    The plan

    Ten actions, none of them a building site

    The brief asked for low-hanging fruit within what is possible for a public institution. Seven of these cost nothing but a decision. The only real spend in the plan is one print run.

    Pick one and run it for a season.

    Our recommendation is the first two together: a mascot the Château already owns, and something for a child to carry round. Both are cheap, both are visible from the gate, and neither needs permission from a ministry.
